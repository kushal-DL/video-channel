# Narration Script — cr-01-pond-half-covered

**Title:** When Was the Pond Half-Covered?
**Series:** Cracked — "Every riddle hides a law. Break it open."
**Hook type:** Real-world scenario (character-grounded)
**Target duration:** 5–7 min
**[MR-UNCERTAIN] flags:** none

---

## HOOK

**Beat 1:**
Day 1: she walks past the pond on her way to the lab. One lily pad, barely the size of her hand. She doesn't stop.

**Beat 2:**
Day 7: a small cluster near the bank. Mildly interesting. She keeps walking.

**Beat 3:**
Day 15: a scatter of pads in the near corner. The water is still mostly clear. No cause for alarm.

**Beat 4:**
Day 25: a quarter of the pond, maybe. She makes a mental note — check back in a few days.

**Beat 5:**
Day 29: she stops. Half the pond is blanketed in green. She stares. When did that happen?

**Beat 6:**
Day 30: she can't see water. The pond covered in one day what had taken 29 days to build toward — and she is right to be surprised.

---

## MOTIVATION

**Beat 7:**
Your first guess is probably Day 15. Thirty days total — split the time in half and the coverage should match. That reasoning works for filling a tub at a steady drip. It fails here. Adding the same amount each day is linear. Doubling each day is exponential — and that changes everything about how the numbers grow.

---

## BUILD-UP

**Beat 8:**
On Day 1, the pad covers one unit of area. Day 2: two units. Day 3: four. Day 4: eight. Each day multiplies the previous by two. [MR]

**Beat 9:**
Track coverage with a formula. The pond is fully covered on Day 30. On Day n, the fraction of the pond covered is 2^n divided by 2^30. [MR]

**Beat 10:**
The last five days tell the story:

Day 25: about 3 percent. Day 26: 6 percent. Day 27: 13 percent. Day 28: 25 percent. Day 29: 50 percent. Day 30: 100 percent. [MR]

Fifty percent on Day 29. The pond crossed the halfway mark with one day to go.

**Beat 11:**
Day 15: 2^15 over 2^30 equals 1 over 2^15 — approximately 0.003 percent. [MR] The scientist barely registered the pond that morning. She was right not to worry.

**Beat 12:**
Why is Day 29 always the answer? The pad doubled from Day 29 to Day 30, so Day 30 is exactly twice Day 29. Day 30 is 100 percent — which forces Day 29 to be exactly 50 percent. [MR] Work one step backward from full. You land on half, every time.

**Beat 13:**
Two panels, side by side. On the left: a pond filling at a steady rate — a straight climb, equal every day. On the right, the same 30-day axis: nearly empty until Day 25, then a surge that swallows the pond in five days. That contrast is sharp. That is the difference between linear and exponential.

---

## RESULT

**Beat 14:**
Day 29. One day before full. The pond sat at exactly 50 percent the morning before it tipped into complete coverage. [MR] That is what the last doubling looks like from the inside.

---

## WORKED EXAMPLE

**Beat 15:**
Back in the lab. The same scientist is culturing bacteria on a glass petri dish. The bacteria double in number every hour. At noon, the dish is completely full. When was it exactly half full?

**Beat 16:**
Her instinct: 6 am. If the bacteria started growing at midnight and the dish is full at noon, six hours in should be halfway — 6 am feels right. Same mistake as the pond.

**Beat 17:**
The bacteria doubled in the last hour — 11 am to noon. The dish was 100 percent full at noon, so at 11 am it was exactly 50 percent full. [MR] One step back. That is the answer.

**Beat 18:**
At 6 am, six steps before noon, the dish held 1 divided by 2^6 of its capacity — under 2 percent. [MR] Practically empty. She would have assumed she had all day.

**Beat 19:**
One law, two containers. Whenever something doubles, the step right before the end is always exactly half the final amount. [MR] Pond, petri dish, anything that multiplies by a fixed factor — the logic is identical.

---

## RECAP

**Beat 20:**
She walks past the pond on Day 30. No water visible. She can reason backward now.

Day 29: half. Day 28: a quarter. Day 27: one-eighth. [MR] Each step back cuts the coverage in half.

**Beat 21:**
The riddle hid the law. Day 15 was a linear answer to an exponential question — it divided the time, not the coverage. The law is this: in any doubling system, the state one step before the end is always exactly half. [MR] A pond. A petri dish. Anywhere things double — you'll see it now.
