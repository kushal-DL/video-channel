# Episode 1: Introduction to Calculus — Brief

**Slug:** `episode-001-introduction-to-calculus`  
**Status:** Ready for script writing

---

## Concept

Calculus is the mathematics of change. This episode introduces derivatives not as an abstract formula, but as a concrete answer to a question every student has asked: "How fast am I going right now?" We'll use the familiar example of a car's speedometer to build intuition before introducing formal notation.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this 6-minute episode, viewers will be able to:

1. Explain what a derivative represents in physical terms (instantaneous rate of change)
2. Distinguish between average velocity and instantaneous velocity
3. Write the derivative notation for position with respect to time using Leibniz notation

---

## Target Audience

- High school AP Calculus students (first exposure)
- Self-learners interested in mathematical thinking
- Engineering undergraduates needing conceptual reinforcement

**Prerequisites:** Algebra II level comfort with functions and graphs. No prior calculus knowledge assumed.

---

## Tone & Style Notes

- **Voice:** Encouraging tutor, not intimidating professor
- **Visuals:** Start concrete (car on road), move abstract (graphs), return to concrete applications
- **Pacing:** Slow at first; accelerate as concepts become familiar
- **Hook:** Open with a speedometer close-up and the question "What does this number really mean?"

---

## Key Math Claims (for math-reviewer)

All mathematical assertions will be tagged `[MR]` in the script. Primary claims:

1. Derivative = limit of average rate of change as interval approaches zero
2. Position function p(t) → velocity v(t) = dp/dt
3. Units of derivative are "units of quantity per unit of time"
